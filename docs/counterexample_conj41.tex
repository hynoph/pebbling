\noindent For a pebbling distribution $P$ on a graph, the \emph{value} of a vertex
$x$ is $v(x) = \sum_y P(y)\,2^{-d(x,y)}$. In~\cite{PPS} the following is
conjectured.

\begin{conjecture}
Let $P$ be a solvable pebbling distribution on the grid. Then for all vertices
$x$ that have degree $4$, $v(x) \ge \frac32$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}
The distribution $P$ in Figure~\ref{fig:cex} on the $10 \times 10$ grid has
$|P| = 57$, is solvable, and satisfies $v(5,5) = \frac{379}{256} < \frac32$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{The value}
Summing the contributions $P(y)\,2^{-d(x,y)}$ of the $24$ occupied vertices
$y$, in row order:
\begin{align*}
v(5,5) = {}&
\tfrac{1}{512} + \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac{3}{64} + \tfrac{1}{64}
+ \tfrac{3}{512} + \tfrac{1}{128} + \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac{3}{64}
+ \tfrac{3}{256} + \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac{1}{32} + \tfrac{3}{32}\\
&+ \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac18 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac18
+ \tfrac14 + \tfrac18 + \tfrac{1}{64} + \tfrac18 + \tfrac{1}{16}
+ \tfrac{3}{64} + \tfrac{1}{64}
\;=\; \frac{379}{256} \;\approx\; 1.4805 .
\end{align*}
It is the only vertex of degree $4$ with value below $\frac32$.

\subsection*{Solvability}
The appendix gives, for each of the $100$ vertices, a sequence of moves from $P$
that places a pebble on it ($466$ moves in total, at most $46$ for one vertex).
A move \texttt{ab}$\to$\texttt{cd} removes two pebbles from $(a,b)$ and adds one
to $(c,d)$, and ``(has a pebble)'' marks vertices that already carry one. Each sequence
can be checked by hand; we have also replayed all of them with three independent
programs.

\subsection*{Larger grids}
We found solvable distributions with a degree-$4$ vertex of value below
$\frac32$ on $n \times n$ grids for $n = 14, 18, 24, 32, 40, 48$, with the
smallest such value settling near $1.4477$, a deficit of about $0.052$, from
$n = 24$ on. These were certified by two independent solvers. In every example
the low vertex lies at distance $6$ from the boundary, so the phenomenon appears
to be tied to the boundary; we do not know whether such vertices exist far from
the boundary. Under the hemmed distribution $P'$ of~\cite{PPS}, every degree-$4$
vertex has value at least $1.97$ in the $10\times10$ to $12\times12$ examples
and about $1.64$ in the larger ones.

\subsection*{How it was found}
By a randomized local search over solvable distributions on small grids, using
moves that compress, split, merge and shift piles of pebbles, with every
candidate's solvability decided exactly by two independent solvers.
